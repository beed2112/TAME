***Tetris*** (Russian: Тетрис) [^2] is a [puzzle video game](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puzzle_video_game "Puzzle video game") created by [Alexey Pajitnov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexey_Pajitnov "Alexey Pajitnov"), a Soviet [software engineer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Software_engineer "Software engineer"), in the mid-1980s. In the game, falling [tetromino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tetromino "Tetromino") shapes must be neatly sorted into a pile. Once a horizontal line of the playfield is filled in, the line disappears, granting points and preventing the pile from reaching the top. Since its creation, this gameplay has been used in [over 220 versions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Tetris_variants "List of Tetris variants"), released for over 70 platforms. Newer versions frequently implement additional game mechanics, some of which have become standard over time. As of December 2024, these versions collectively serve as the [second-best-selling video game series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_best-selling_video_game_franchises "List of best-selling video game franchises") with over 520 million sales, mostly on mobile devices.

In the mid-1980s, Pajitnov created *Tetris* in his spare time while working at the [Dorodnitsyn Computing Center of the Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dorodnitsyn_Computing_Center_of_the_Academy_of_Sciences "Dorodnitsyn Computing Center of the Academy of Sciences"), initially programming it in [Pascal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pascal_\(programming_language\) "Pascal (programming language)") for the [Elektronika 60](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elektronika_60 "Elektronika 60") in about three weeks, then spent over two months [porting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porting "Porting") it to the [IBM PC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IBM_PC "IBM PC") using [Turbo Pascal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turbo_Pascal "Turbo Pascal"), with help from Dmitry Pavlovsky and [Vadim Gerasimov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vadim_Gerasimov "Vadim Gerasimov"). [Floppy disk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Floppy_disk "Floppy disk") copies were distributed freely throughout Moscow before spreading to Eastern Europe. Robert Stein of Andromeda Software saw the game in Hungary and contacted the Dorodnitsyn Computing Center to secure a license to release the game commercially. Stein then sublicensed to [Mirrorsoft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mirrorsoft "Mirrorsoft") in the UK and [Spectrum HoloByte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spectrum_HoloByte "Spectrum HoloByte") in the US. [Both companies released the game](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tetris_\(Spectrum_HoloByte\) "Tetris (Spectrum HoloByte)") in 1988 to commercial success and sublicensed to additional companies, including [Henk Rogers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henk_Rogers "Henk Rogers") ' [Bullet-Proof Software](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bullet-Proof_Software "Bullet-Proof Software"). Rogers negotiated with [Elektronorgtechnica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elektronorgtechnica "Elektronorgtechnica"), the state-owned organization in charge of licensing Soviet software, to license *Tetris* to [Nintendo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nintendo "Nintendo") for the [Game Boy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Game_Boy "Game Boy") and [Nintendo Entertainment System](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nintendo_Entertainment_System "Nintendo Entertainment System") (NES); both versions were released in 1989.

With 35 million sales as of June 2024, the [Game Boy version](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tetris_\(Game_Boy\) "Tetris (Game Boy)") is the best-selling version of *Tetris* and among the [best-selling video games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Best-selling_video_games "Best-selling video games") of all time. Its commercial success upon release contributed to the Game Boy's success and popularized *Tetris*. At the end of 1995, Dorodnitsyn Computing Center's rights to *Tetris*, arranged ten years prior, reverted to Pajitnov. He and Rogers subsequently formed [the Tetris Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Tetris_Company "The Tetris Company") to manage licensing. Guidelines for authorized releases were established, with certain features not in the original games becoming standardized over time. Versions of *Tetris* were released on mobile devices starting in the 2000s, with [Electronic Arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electronic_Arts "Electronic Arts") (EA) holding a license on [such ports](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tetris_\(EA\) "Tetris (EA)") from 2006 to 2020, to widespread commercial success. *Tetris* received renewed popularity in the late-2010s with the release of the critically successful *[Tetris Effect](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tetris_Effect "Tetris Effect")* (2018) and *[Tetris 99](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tetris_99 "Tetris 99")* (2019).

*Tetris* is frequently cited as one of the [greatest and most influential games ever made](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_video_games_considered_the_best "List of video games considered the best"), and was among the inaugural class inducted into the [World Video Game Hall of Fame](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Video_Game_Hall_of_Fame "World Video Game Hall of Fame") in 2015. Its gameplay has been influential in the genre of puzzle video games, being cited as an early example of [casual gaming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Casual_gaming "Casual gaming"). Furthermore, *Tetris* has been represented in a vast array of media such as architecture and art and been the subject of academic research, including studies of its potential for psychological intervention. A competitive culture has formed around *Tetris*, particularly [the NES version](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tetris_\(NES_video_game\) "Tetris (NES video game)"), with players – typically adolescents – competing at the annual [Classic Tetris World Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Classic_Tetris_World_Championship "Classic Tetris World Championship"). [A film dramatization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tetris_\(film\) "Tetris (film)") of its development was released in 2023.

## Gameplay

![In a black, upright rectangle bordered by gray squares, multiple tetrominoes of different colors are stacked on top one another, leaving a gap to the right that an I-shaped tetromino is about to enter vertically.](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/9c/Typical_Tetris_Game.svg/250px-Typical_Tetris_Game.svg.png)

A typical Tetris game screen

Across its numerous versions, *Tetris* generally has a consistent [puzzle video game](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puzzle_video_game "Puzzle video game") design.[^10] Gameplay consists of a rectangular field in which [tetromino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tetromino "Tetromino") pieces,[^3] geometric shapes consisting of four connected squares, descend from the top-center. During the descent, the player can move the piece horizontally and rotate them until they touch the bottom of the field or another piece.[^12] [^13] The player's goal is to stack the pieces in the field to create horizontal lines of blocks.[^14] When a line is completed, it disappears and the blocks placed above fall one row. The speed of the descending pieces increases as lines are cleared. The game ends if the accumulated pieces in the field block other pieces from entering the field, a process known as "topping out".[^13] Common mechanics among *Tetris* versions include the queue (viewing the pieces that are next to appear), soft drop (increasing the descent of the piece), hard drop (instantly placing the piece as far down as it can go), and holding (reserving a piece for later use).[^14]

The objective of *Tetris* is to collect as many points as possible during a gameplay session by clearing lines.[^12] *Tetris* 's scoring system has remained mostly consistent since *[Tetris DS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tetris_DS "Tetris DS")* (2006) with some exceptions. Points gained during gameplay increase with the descent speed. The more lines cleared at once, the higher the score for a line clear. Completing four horizontal lines at once by placing an I-shaped tetromino into a single-column gap is referred to as a "Tetris"; most versions award the highest score for it. The player can also gain points by using hard drops or soft drops.[^14] Advanced techniques, often used in competitive play, include T-spins (spinning a T-shaped tetromino into a blocked gap), perfect clears (emptying the field following a line clear), and combos (clearing lines consecutively).[^15]

## History

### Creation (1984–1985)

![A man](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/1/12/Alexey_Pajitnov_January_2008_cropped.jpg)

Alexey Pajitnov (pictured in 2008), the creator of Tetris

[Alexey Pajitnov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexey_Pajitnov "Alexey Pajitnov") was a [speech recognition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Speech_recognition "Speech recognition") and [artificial intelligence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artificial_intelligence "Artificial intelligence") researcher at the [Dorodnitsyn Computing Center of the Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dorodnitsyn_Computing_Center_of_the_Academy_of_Sciences "Dorodnitsyn Computing Center of the Academy of Sciences").[^16] While at the center, he developed more than twenty types of games in his spare time [^17]<sup><span title="Page: 72">: 72 </span></sup>  on the [Elektronika 60](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elektronika_60 "Elektronika 60"),[^18] a Russian clone of the [PDP-11](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PDP-11 "PDP-11") computer.[^19] He became inspired to convert [pentomino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pentomino "Pentomino") tiling puzzles to the computer [^20] after he bought a pentomino puzzle set from a store and played with it in his office.[^21] Sources vary on the exact timing: certain sources indicate June 1984,[^20] while the BBC documentary *Tetris: From Russia with Love* (2004) suggests spring 1985.[^22]

Pajitnov programmed *Tetris* using [Pascal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pascal_\(programming_language\) "Pascal (programming language)") for the [RT-11](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RT-11 "RT-11") operating system on the Elektronika 60 [^23] and experimented with different versions.[^24] Because the Elektronika 60 had no [graphical interface](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Graphical_user_interface "Graphical user interface"), Pajitnov modeled the field and pieces using spaces and brackets.[^24] He felt that the game would be needlessly complicated with the twelve different shapes of pentominoes, so he scaled the concept down to [tetrominoes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tetromino "Tetromino"), of which there are only seven shapes.[^20] Afterward, he programmed the basic mechanics, including the ability to flip tetrominoes as they fell in a vertical screen and the clearing of lines.[^20] [^25] [^26] The name *Tetris* was a combination of "tetra" (meaning "four") and Pajitnov's favorite sport, [tennis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tennis "Tennis").[^27] [^28]

Pajitnov completed the first version of *Tetris*, though sources differ on the timeline. In a 1993 interview with the Japanese magazine *The Super Famicom*, he recalled that the game was finished in 1985 and said it took about three weeks to develop due to its simplicity,[^17]<sup><span title="Page: 72">: 72 </span></sup>  while Henk Rogers reported in 2024 that Pajitnov recalled first developing it on the Elektronika 60 in 1984.[^29] This version had no scoring system and no levels,[^30] but it nonetheless captivated Pajitnov's peers.[^31] [^32]

Pajitnov sought to [port](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porting "Porting") *Tetris* to the [IBM Personal Computer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IBM_Personal_Computer "IBM Personal Computer") (IBM PC), which had a higher-quality display than the Elektronika 60. He recruited his colleague Dmitry Pavlovsky and the 16-year-old computer prodigy [Vadim Gerasimov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vadim_Gerasimov "Vadim Gerasimov").[^33] [^34] Using [Turbo Pascal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turbo_Pascal "Turbo Pascal"),[^23] the three adapted *Tetris* to the IBM PC over two months, with Gerasimov incorporating color and Pavlovsky incorporating a scoreboard.[^33] [^35] Floppy disk copies of this version were distributed freely throughout the Dorodnitsyn Computing Center, before spreading quickly among Moscow computer circles.[^36] Pajitnov kept note of second-hand accounts of *Tetris* 's spread during this time.[^37] *Tetris* reportedly won second place in a [Zelenodolsk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zelenodolsk,_Republic_of_Tatarstan "Zelenodolsk, Republic of Tatarstan") computer game competition in November 1985,[^33] and by 1986, nearly everyone with an IBM computer in Moscow and several major cities had played *Tetris*.[^38]

![Gameplay of Tetris, represented by green ASCII text due to the system's primitive nature](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/en/7/7c/Tetris-VeryFirstVersion.png)

The first Tetris version, developed for the Elektronika 60 in the mid-1980s, is shown here running in emulation.

Under Soviet law, intellectual rights were not protected, and the state-run organization [Elektronorgtechnica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elektronorgtechnica "Elektronorgtechnica") (Elorg) had a monopoly on the import and export of software.[^39] Around this time, Pajitnov arranged for the Dorodnitsyn Computing Center to have the rights to *Tetris* for ten years to ease potential legal troubles.[^40] As a result, Pajitnov could not sell *Tetris* for profit. Nonetheless, Pajitnov's manager Victor Brjabrin liked *Tetris* and sought opportunities for success beyond the Soviet Union.[^41] [^42] In early 1986, Brjabrin sent a copy of *Tetris* to the SZKI Institute of Computer Studies in [Budapest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Budapest "Budapest").[^41] [^42] Robert Stein, founder of Andromeda Software who profited by licensing software from Hungary to UK companies, encountered *Tetris* during a visit to the SZKI Institute and found its gameplay compelling.[^43] Stein learned from the SZKI Institute director that a researcher there, Gábor Bolgár, had ported *Tetris* to the [Commodore 64](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commodore_64 "Commodore 64").[^44] He returned to [London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London "London") and contacted Dorodnitsyn Computing Center by [telex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Telex "Telex") to obtain the license rights, believing he could sell those rights to a larger UK publisher.[^45]

Brjabrin received the telex and, after translating it from English to Russian, disclosed it to Pajitnov, who spent days attempting to compose, translate to English, and send a favorable yet noncommittal response by telex to Stein. Despite this attempt, Stein interpreted the response as granting him the license and proceeded to find a publisher for *Tetris*.[^46] Stein pitched *Tetris* to [Jim Mackonochie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jim_Mackonochie "Jim Mackonochie") of [Mirrorsoft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mirrorsoft "Mirrorsoft"), a UK software company founded by business magnate [Robert Maxwell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Maxwell "Robert Maxwell") and Mackonochie. Though Mackonochie was skeptical about the commercial potential of *Tetris*, he consulted Phil Adam, president of US sister company Spectrum HoloByte, for his input. During his overseas visit to Mirrorsoft, Adam played *Tetris* for hours and then encouraged Mackonochie to accept Stein's offer. Though still cautious, Mackonochie agreed to allow himself the licensing rights for Europe and Adam the rights for the United States and Japan.[^47] Stein sold the rights to the two companies for £3,000 and royalties of 7.5–15% of sales, even though negotiations with the Dorodnitsyn Computing Center were at a standstill, with the Dorodnitsyn Computing Center being resistant to selling *Tetris* in the West.[^48]

[Gilman Louie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gilman_Louie "Gilman Louie"), CEO of Spectrum HoloByte, sought to exoticize the game's Soviet origins, marketing it as the first Soviet product to be sold in North America, alongside implementing Soviet folk music and imagery during gameplay and using red packaging adorned with an illustration of [Saint Basil's Cathedral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Basil%27s_Cathedral "Saint Basil's Cathedral").[^49] [^50] *Tetris* was [first commercially released](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tetris_\(Spectrum_HoloByte\) "Tetris (Spectrum HoloByte)") in the West for the IBM PC, with ports to other computer systems planned for release in the following weeks. Mirrorsoft released the game in the United Kingdom on January 27, 1988,[^51] and Spectrum HoloByte released it in the United States on January 29, 1988.[^52] Gábor Bolgár sold his [Commodore 64](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commodore_64 "Commodore 64") port to Stein's Andromeda Software, who polished it with additional sound work before licensing it to Mirrorsoft.[^53] Mirrorsoft rewrote the code of the original IBM release for systems such as the [Amiga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amiga "Amiga") and [Atari ST](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atari_ST "Atari ST").[^54] Boosted by [word of mouth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Word_of_mouth "Word of mouth") and positive reviews,[^55] [^56] this *Tetris* release was commercially successful, selling 100 thousand copies within a year.[^57] At the [Software Publishers Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Software_and_Information_Industry_Association "Software and Information Industry Association") 's Excellence in Software Awards ceremony in 1989, *Tetris* won across three categories.[^58]

At the time, the only document certifying a license fee was the telex from Pajitnov and Brjabrin, meaning that Stein had sold the license for a game he did not yet own. Additionally, Alexander Alexinko, director of Elorg, discovered Stein's negotiations with Pajitnov and Dorodnitsyn Computing Center and assessed their communications with disapproval. In response, Elorg took over representing the Soviet Union in negotiations.[^59] Through communications, Alexinko attempted to revoke any potential deal the Soviet Union might have had with Stein in favor of having Elorg itself sell *Tetris* internationally. Stein responded by threatening to create a scandal that would harm the Soviet Union's international standing, persuading Alexinko to consider negotiating the rights to *Tetris*.[^60] An agreement was drafted by the end of February 1988 and finalized by May, granting Stein the rights to *Tetris* on computers.[^61] [^60]

![A man](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/ab/Henk_Rogers_face_crop.png/330px-Henk_Rogers_face_crop.png)

Henk Rogers (pictured in 2010), representing Nintendo, negotiated with Elorg for the rights to Tetris.

Following the commercial release of *Tetris*, Spectrum HoloByte and Mirrorsoft started licensing the game to other companies for platforms that were not covered by the contract that Stein and Elorg had agreed to.[^62] At the time, [Henk Rogers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henk_Rogers "Henk Rogers") had been seeking video games across the world to sell in Japan through his company [Bullet-Proof Software](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bullet-Proof_Software "Bullet-Proof Software"); he discovered *Tetris* as a publicly displayed video game at the 1988 Winter [Consumer Electronics Show](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Consumer_Electronics_Show "Consumer Electronics Show") (CES).[^63] Mirrorsoft sold [Atari Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atari_Games "Atari Games") subsidiary [Tengen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tengen_\(company\) "Tengen (company)") the rights to sell non-computer releases of *Tetris* in Japan in exchange for the rights to sell computer ports of *[Blasteroids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blasteroids "Blasteroids")* worldwide.[^64] Afterward, Tengen sold the Japanese arcade rights to [Sega](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sega "Sega") and the console rights to Rogers, who received the Japanese computer rights from Spectrum HoloByte.[^65] [^66] Bullet-Proof Software released *Tetris* for Japanese computers in November 1988 and [Nintendo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nintendo "Nintendo") 's [Family Computer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Family_Computer "Family Computer") (Famicom) in December 1988, the latter of which became commercially successful, selling two million copies in Japan.[^67] Despite this, Elorg was unaware of the Famicom version and was receiving no royalties from *Tetris* 's worldwide success.[^68] [^69]

Around the time of *Tetris* 's Famicom version, Nintendo developed the [Game Boy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Game_Boy "Game Boy"), an economical handheld game console that interested Nintendo president [Hiroshi Yamauchi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hiroshi_Yamauchi "Hiroshi Yamauchi") and [Nintendo of America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nintendo_of_America "Nintendo of America") president [Minoru Arakawa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minoru_Arakawa "Minoru Arakawa").[^70] Arakawa, aided by Nintendo of America vice president [Howard Lincoln](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Howard_Lincoln "Howard Lincoln"), sought to port *Tetris* to the Game Boy, believing that it would be a commercial success. They were both discouraged by the convoluted nature of the game's legal rights, leading Arakawa to enlist Rogers in getting the handheld rights to *Tetris*.[^71] Rogers contacted Stein by [fax](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fax "Fax") in November 15, 1988, for the handheld rights, with Stein responding that he was negotiating them with Elorg. At the time, Alexinko had been replaced by the more adversarial Evgeni Belikov as director of Elorg. After failing to get the rights after multiple attempts at contacting Stein, Rogers abandoned him in favor of negotiating directly with the Soviet government.[^72] In February 1989, Rogers traveled to the Soviet Union and arrived at the Elorg offices uninvited to negotiate the rights. Discussions with Rogers were scheduled the next day.[^73]

While trying to persuade Elorg to grant him the handheld rights, Rogers displayed a Famicom *Tetris* [cartridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Game_cartridge "Game cartridge") to demonstrate the game's success. Belikov did not recognize the cartridge, believing that the rights to *Tetris* had only been signed for computer systems per the contract with Stein, and accused Rogers of illegal publication.[^74] [^75] Though surprised, Rogers provided a check of over $40,000 to Elorg as to remedy this breach of contract and discussed granting him the console rights. Afterward, Belikov recognized the potential financial benefits of allying with Rogers over the other incoming negotiators: Stein and Robert's son [Kevin Maxwell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kevin_Maxwell "Kevin Maxwell").[^76] While Rogers was consulting Nintendo for a potential offer to Elorg, Belikov implicitly diminished Stein and Kevin's standing in subsequent meetings by coercing the former into signing an updated contract with an exclusionary definition of computers and baiting the latter into admitting that Mirrosoft did not have the console rights, unaware of the commercial Famicom release.[^77] Rogers, alongside Arakawa and Lincoln, returned to Moscow, and after a few days of negotiation, Nintendo received both the handheld and console rights to *Tetris* from Elorg.[^78]

![Refer to caption](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/9d/Tetris_on_Game_Boy.jpg/330px-Tetris_on_Game_Boy.jpg)

Tetris running on a Nintendo Game Boy

On March 31, 1989, taking advantage of the new agreement, Lincoln sent a [cease and desist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cease_and_desist "Cease and desist") fax to Hideyuki Nakajima, president of Atari Games, concerning their subsidiary [Tengen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tengen_\(company\) "Tengen (company)") 's production of *Tetris* for the Nintendo Entertainment System (NES), the North American equivalent to the Famicom.[^79] Believing themselves to own the console rights to *Tetris*, Tengen filed copyright applications for the game in the United States and preemptively sued Nintendo.[^80] [^79] Meanwhile, after being informed by Kevin, Robert pressured Elorg for sidestepping prior agreements with his companies by contacting ministers from both the Soviet and UK governments.[^81] In an in-person meeting, Robert informed Soviet General Secretary [Mikhail Gorbachev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mikhail_Gorbachev "Mikhail Gorbachev") of his situation regarding *Tetris*, to which he responded that Robert "should no longer worry about the Japanese company".[^82] [^83] Despite pressure from both Robert and the Soviet government, Belikov stated his refusal to concede in a conversation with Lincoln.[^84]

After a marketing campaign ordered by Nakajima, Tengen released [their version of *Tetris*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tetris_\(Tengen\) "Tetris (Tengen)") on the NES on May 17, 1989, selling tens of thousands of copies within a few weeks.[^85] The next month, Judge [Fern M. Smith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fern_M._Smith "Fern M. Smith") of the [US District Court for the Northern District of California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/US_District_Court_for_the_Northern_District_of_California "US District Court for the Northern District of California") presided over competing lawsuits from Atari Games and Nintendo over their console rights to *Tetris*.[^86] Both companies motioned for preliminary injunctions that would prohibit the other company from selling *Tetris*. On June 15, 1989, in defense of its motion for injunction, Atari Games argued that the NES fell under the definition of computers under the original contract and that Elorg only excluded consoles from its definition of computers to take advantage of higher profits from Nintendo. Based on contradicting evidence, Smith rejected this argument and declared that Mirrorsoft and Spectrum HoloByte had never received explicit authorization for marketing on consoles, granting a preliminary injunction against Atari Games on June 22.[^86]

The next day, Atari Games withdrew its NES version from sale, and thousands of cartridges remained unsold in its warehouses. Preference for this release over Nintendo's led to Atari Games cartridges selling for up to $300 on the [secondary market](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Video_game_collecting "Video game collecting").[^87] [The Game Boy version of *Tetris*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tetris_\(Game_Boy_video_game\) "Tetris (Game Boy video game)") was released in Japan on June 14, 1989,[^88] and as a [pack-in game](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pack-in_game "Pack-in game") in the United States on July 31, 1989.[^89] [^90] [Nintendo's NES version](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tetris_\(NES_video_game\) "Tetris (NES video game)") was released the same year. Both releases achieved commercial success.[^91] The Game Boy version was the primary game promoted for the Game Boy, becoming its [killer app](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Killer_app "Killer app"),[^92] generating $80 million in revenue,[^93] and popularizing both the Game Boy and the *Tetris* game.[^94] [^95] The NES version quickly sold three million copies and appeared on Nintendo's most popular games list for over a year.[^90] [^96]

On November 13, 1989, Smith ended the legal battle between Nintendo and Atari Games regarding *Tetris* by [summary judgment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Summary_judgment "Summary judgment"), granting Nintendo the console rights to *Tetris*.[^97] In 1991, with Rogers' help, Pajitnov and his family emigrated to [Seattle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seattle "Seattle"), where he worked as a freelance game designer.[^98] [^99] During this time, Pajitnov worked on several sequels to *Tetris*. *[Welltris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Welltris "Welltris")* (1990) involved adjusting geometrical pieces descending down one of four walls of a three-dimensional well, and *[Hatris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hatris "Hatris")* (1990) and *[Faces...tris III](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Faces...tris_III "Faces...tris III")* (1991) replaced descending tetrominoes with hats and faces respectively.[^100] Though they generally received positive reviews and commercial success,[^99] with *Faces...Tris III* winning "Best Action/Arcade Program" in the 1991 Excellence in Software Awards,[^101] none replicated *Tetris* ' success.[^102] [^103] Other early versions and sequels of *Tetris* were developed without Pajitnov's involvement, including Spectrum Holobyte's *[Super Tetris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Super_Tetris "Super Tetris")* (1991), Bullet-Proof Software's *[Tetris 2 + BomBliss](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tetris_2_%2B_BomBliss "Tetris 2 + BomBliss")* (1991) and *[Tetris Battle Gaiden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tetris_Battle_Gaiden "Tetris Battle Gaiden")* (1993), and Nintendo's *[Tetris 2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tetris_2_\(1993_video_game\) "Tetris 2 (1993 video game)")* (1993).[^100]

A 5th generation iPod running Tetris (2006)

The Dorodnitsyn Computing Center's rights to *Tetris* expired at the end of 1995, reverting back to Pajitnov.[^40] Worried that Elorg, which had become a private company under Belikov following the 1991 [collapse of the Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Collapse_of_the_Soviet_Union "Collapse of the Soviet Union"),[^98] would try to claim the rights, Pajitnov recruited Rogers to secure them. In 1995, Rogers formed [Blue Planet Software](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blue_Planet_Software "Blue Planet Software"); in 1996, he established [The Tetris Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Tetris_Company "The Tetris Company") as an equal partnership between Elorg and Blue Planet Software. Rogers acquired Elorg and renamed it [Tetris Holding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tetris_Holding "Tetris Holding") in 2005.[^104] [^105] Since its formation, the Tetris Company has maintained guidelines for authorized versions of *Tetris*, and Blue Planet Software has served as an agent for the *Tetris* brand.[^100]<sup><span title="Page: 25">: 25 </span></sup>  The Tetris Company has also enforced its copyright of the *Tetris* game against unauthorized clones,[^106] [^107] such as in the 2012 case *[Tetris Holding, LLC v. Xio Interactive, Inc.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tetris_Holding,_LLC_v._Xio_Interactive,_Inc. "Tetris Holding, LLC v. Xio Interactive, Inc.")*, where a judge ruled that the iOS game *Mino* violated *Tetris* 's copyright based on [look and feel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Look_and_feel "Look and feel").[^108]

Pajitnov and Rogers sought to keep *Tetris* versions fresh, and innovated in new directions. *[Tetrisphere](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tetrisphere "Tetrisphere")*, developed by H2O Entertainment and released on August 11, 1997, was an example of this innovation.[^100] [^109] Gameplay involved rotating a three-dimensional sphere to place pieces on its surface. It was the first puzzle video game on the [Nintendo 64](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nintendo_64 "Nintendo 64") and garnered a [cult following](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cult_following "Cult following").[^110] [^111] David Crookes of *[Retro Gamer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Retro_Gamer "Retro Gamer")* called *Tetrisphere* "proof that the concept could be modernised and tweaked, while still being faithful to the original".[^100] Another version on the Nintendo 64, the Japan-exclusive *[Tetris 64](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tetris_64 "Tetris 64")* (1998), allowed for four players and was the only game to utilize the Nintendo 64's Bio Sensor, which detected a player's pulse.[^100] [^112] On other platforms around this time, *[Tetris Plus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tetris_Plus "Tetris Plus")* (1996), *[Tetris DX](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tetris_DX "Tetris DX")* (1998), and *[The Next Tetris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Next_Tetris "The Next Tetris")* (1999) added new game modes, and *[Tetris: The Grand Master](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tetris:_The_Grand_Master "Tetris: The Grand Master")* (1998) was an arcade game targeted toward experienced players.[^100]

According to Rogers, in order to appeal to beginner players, the Tetris Company started to incorporate features not in the original releases into the *Tetris* guidelines.[^113] These features included the hold feature and ability to perform both soft drops and hard drops in *[The New Tetris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_New_Tetris "The New Tetris")* in 1999,[^114] the easy spin and super rotation system in *[Tetris Worlds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tetris_Worlds "Tetris Worlds")* in 2001,[^115] and the scoring system introduced in *Tetris DS* in 2006.[^14] Critics panned *Tetris Worlds* for the easy spin mechanic, which allowed players to delay a piece's descent by continually rotating it. Despite the controversy and Pajitnov's reluctance, the mechanic was implemented into the *Tetris* guidelines.[^116] [^113] *Tetris Worlds* also introduced the super rotation system, defining how pieces are to rotate,[^115] which most *Tetris* games have since used.[^14]

*Tetris* was first released on mobile devices in 2001 by [G-Mode](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G-Mode "G-Mode").[^117] In 2002, Rogers formed Blue Lava Wireless to develop *Tetris* games for mobile platforms.[^100] JAMDAT acquired Blue Lava Wireless in April 2005, granting the former a 15-year license of *Tetris* for mobile platforms.[^118] By December 2005, when [Electronic Arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electronic_Arts "Electronic Arts") (EA) started its acquisition of JAMDAT, *Tetris* had been consistently selling well on American carrier phones.[^119] EA completed its acquisition in February 2006, granting it the mobile license for *Tetris*.[^120] [EA Mobile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EA_Mobile "EA Mobile") released their [mobile version of *Tetris*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tetris_\(Electronic_Arts\) "Tetris (Electronic Arts)") as a launch game for the [iTunes store](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ITunes_store "ITunes store") on [iPod 5G](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IPod_5G "IPod 5G") on September 11, 2006,[^121] [^122] and on the [Apple App Store](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apple_App_Store "Apple App Store") on [iOS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IOS "IOS") on July 10, 2008.[^123] By January 2010, EA's mobile releases reached 100 million paid downloads, making *Tetris* the most popular mobile game at the time.[^124]

Maya Rogers (pictured in 2022) succeeded her father as CEO of Blue Planet Software in January 2014.

In January 2014, after eight years of involvement, Henk Rogers' daughter [Maya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maya_Rogers "Maya Rogers") succeeded him as the CEO of Blue Planet Software.[^4] She began by planning activities for *Tetris* 's 30th anniversary. In an interview with *[VentureBeat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/VentureBeat "VentureBeat")* in June 2014, Maya spoke of her desire to expand *Tetris* 's brand, such as through merchandising, and keeping the game fresh.[^127] [Sega](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sega "Sega") released *[Puyo Puyo Tetris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puyo_Puyo_Tetris "Puyo Puyo Tetris")*, a crossover between *Tetris* and *[Puyo Puyo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puyo_Puyo "Puyo Puyo")*, in Japan on February 6, 2014, for multiple platforms.[^128] *Puyo Puyo Tetris* sold over 60,000 copies within a week, with the [Nintendo 3DS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nintendo_3DS "Nintendo 3DS") port being the second-highest-selling game of the week according to *[4Gamer.net](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/4Gamer.net "4Gamer.net")*.[^129] [Ubisoft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ubisoft "Ubisoft") 's *[Tetris Ultimate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tetris_Ultimate "Tetris Ultimate")* was released on the [Nintendo 3DS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nintendo_3DS "Nintendo 3DS") in November 2014 and the [PlayStation 4](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PlayStation_4 "PlayStation 4") and [Xbox One](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xbox_One "Xbox One") in December 2014.[^130] *Tetris Ultimate* received mixed reviews, though the online multiplayer was generally seen favorably.[^131] [^100]

In the late 2010s, the *Tetris* series had a resurgence in popularity with the release of *[Tetris Effect](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tetris_Effect "Tetris Effect")* and *[Tetris 99](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tetris_99 "Tetris 99")*.[^5] *[PCMag](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PCMag "PCMag")* credited the resurgence to the 2017 release of *Puyo Puyo Tetris* in the Western world,[^133] where it received positive reviews and sold 1.4 million copies worldwide by November 2020.[^138] [^139] *Tetris Effect*, developed by Monstars and Resonair and published by Enhance Games,[^140] was released on the [PlayStation 4](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PlayStation_4 "PlayStation 4") on November 9, 2018,[^141] and on Windows on July 23, 2019,[^142] receiving widespread critical acclaim for its visuals and emotional impact.[^132] [^143] [^144] *Tetris 99* is a [battle royale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_royale_game "Battle royale game") version of *Tetris* made available to subscribers of [Nintendo Switch Online](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nintendo_Switch_Online "Nintendo Switch Online") on February 13, 2019, upon its surprise announcement during [Nintendo Direct](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nintendo_Direct "Nintendo Direct").[^145] It received positive reviews and became Nintendo Switch Online's killer app;[^132] [^133] [^146] according to President of Nintendo [Shuntaro Furukawa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shuntaro_Furukawa "Shuntaro Furukawa"), 2.8 million Nintendo Switch Online users played *Tetris 99* within a few months of release.[^147]

EA announced in January 2020 that its license for mobile releases of *Tetris* would expire on April 21, 2020, with the game becoming inoperable as a result.[^148] Video game developer N3twork subsequently released authorized mobile releases on the iOS and Android on January 23, 2020.[^149] These accumulated 30 million downloads before [social casino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_network_game "Social network game") company [PlayStudios](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Playstudios "Playstudios") acquired the rights to them in November 2021.[^150] On March 28, 2023, Playstudios incorporated a playAwards [loyalty program](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Loyalty_program "Loyalty program") onto the *Tetris* mobile apps, allowing players to win points for playing *Tetris* that can be redeemed for real-life awards.[^151] *[Tetris Forever](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tetris_Forever "Tetris Forever")*, a compilation of *Tetris* games and [interactive documentary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interactive_documentary "Interactive documentary") developed by [Digital Eclipse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Digital_Eclipse "Digital Eclipse"), was released on November 12, 2024,[^152] to positive reviews for chronicling the history of *Tetris*.[^153] [^154]

## Versions

*Tetris* has been released on a multitude of platforms since its initial creation. It is available on many game consoles, personal computers, smartphones, among other platforms. To date, *[Guinness World Records](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guinness_World_Records "Guinness World Records")* recognizes *Tetris* as the video game with the most ports, totaling over 220 versions across over 70 platforms.[^155] [^156] Across its multiple versions, *Tetris* 's core gameplay has remained consistent.[^10] Since 1996, the Tetris Company has maintained annual standard specifications for authorized versions of *Tetris*.[^30] [^113] Pajitnov considers these guidelines a baseline for different versions and not "set in stone".[^157] Several game mechanics of *Tetris* have been changed over time. For example, the distribution of tetrominoes was completely randomized in early versions, while modern versions use a "bag system", in which each tetromino is guaranteed to appear once in a set of seven.[^158] [^159] Other mechanics that have become standardized in modern versions include the ability to hold tetrominoes to swap with later pieces, introduced in *[The New Tetris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_New_Tetris "The New Tetris")* (1999),[^160] and the super rotation system and infinite spin, introduced in *[Tetris Worlds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tetris_Worlds "Tetris Worlds")* (2001).[^115]

The original Elektronika 60 version of *Tetris* had no music.[^161] Spectrum Holobyte's version of *Tetris* in the United States exoticized the Soviet origins through elements such as Russian music, including [Pyotr Ilyich Tchaikovsky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pyotr_Ilyich_Tchaikovsky "Pyotr Ilyich Tchaikovsky") 's " [Trepak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trepak_\(The_Nutcracker\) "Trepak (The Nutcracker)") " from *[The Nutcracker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Nutcracker "The Nutcracker")* and [Reinhold Glière](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reinhold_Gli%C3%A8re "Reinhold Glière") 's " [Russian Sailor Dance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Sailor_Dance "Russian Sailor Dance") " from *[The Red Poppy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Red_Poppy "The Red Poppy")*. This approach differed from other versions of *Tetris* from other countries at the time: Mirrorsoft's Commodore 64 versions in Europe used an atmospheric soundtrack, and Sega's arcade version in Japan used a synthesized pop-influenced soundtrack.[^162] Nintendo's versions for NES and Game Boy continued the pattern of using Russian music. The NES version uses Tchaikovsky's " [Dance of the Sugar Plum Fairy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dance_of_the_Sugar_Plum_Fairy "Dance of the Sugar Plum Fairy") " from *The Nutcracker* as Music A, with the Russian-influenced Music B and the mellow Music C having unclear origins.[^163] The Game Boy version has the 1860s Russian folk tune " [Korobeiniki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korobeiniki "Korobeiniki") " for Music A, an original composition by [Hirokazu Tanaka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hirokazu_Tanaka "Hirokazu Tanaka") for Music B, and the Menuet of [Johann Sebastian Bach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johann_Sebastian_Bach "Johann Sebastian Bach") 's *[French Suite no. 3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Suites_\(Bach\) "French Suites (Bach)")* for Music C.[^164] "Korobeiniki" has become primarily associated with *Tetris* as its main theme and would be used in most significant versions within the series,[^165] [^162] as mandated by the Tetris Company guidelines.[^30]

## Legacy

### Industry impact

Due to Rogers and Nintendo's belief in its potential for mass appeal, *Tetris* was the pack-in game and the primary game promoted for the Game Boy in the United States. The resulting public anticipation led *Tetris* to become the Game Boy's main draw,[^92] [^225] [^226] with many, including non-gamers, buying the Game Boy specifically to play *Tetris*. This release simultaneously contributed to both the popularity of the *Tetris* game and the Game Boy,[^227] [^95] [^228] with the bundle selling out its initial run of a million copies shortly after release.[^92] This success established Nintendo's dominant position in the [handheld gaming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Handheld_gaming "Handheld gaming") market, setting a standard that competitors struggled to replicate.[^212] [^229] [^230] Several writers credit *Tetris* and *[Pokémon Red and Blue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pok%C3%A9mon_Red_and_Blue "Pokémon Red and Blue")* (1996) for the Game Boy's longevity, as it would not be discontinued until 2003.[^228] [^92] [^231]

*Tetris* is influential in the genre of [puzzle video games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puzzle_video_games "Puzzle video games").[^211] [^232] Commentators have considered *Tetris* an early example of a [casual game](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Casual_game "Casual game").[^8] *[Wired](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wired_\(magazine\) "Wired (magazine)")* deemed *Tetris* unique for its time, given its appeal to players regardless of gender and age,[^234] and *1Up.com* credits *Tetris* for establishing a market for puzzle video games with universal appeal.[^212] Various common elements of puzzle games, such as managing pieces over a fixed screen, originated from *Tetris*,[^211] and multiple clones have been created to replicate *Tetris* 's popularity.[^237] Video games influenced by *Tetris* include Nintendo's *[Dr. Mario](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dr._Mario "Dr. Mario")* (1990), Sega's *[Columns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columns_\(video_game\) "Columns (video game)")* (1990), [Compile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compile_\(company\) "Compile (company)") 's *[Puyo Puyo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puyo_Puyo_\(video_game\) "Puyo Puyo (video game)")* (1991), [Taito](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taito "Taito") 's *[Puzzle Bobble](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puzzle_Bobble "Puzzle Bobble")* (1994), and [Capcom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capcom "Capcom") 's *[Super Puzzle Fighter II Turbo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Super_Puzzle_Fighter_II_Turbo "Super Puzzle Fighter II Turbo")* (1996).[^9]

### Cultural impact

![A man holding a trophy](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/f0/Jonas_Neubauer_crop_1.jpg/330px-Jonas_Neubauer_crop_1.jpg)

Jonas Neubauer (pictured in 2010) won the inaugural Classic Tetris World Championship.

*Tetris* 's cultural impact and recognition is widespread, being represented in a vast array of video game platforms, among other media such as architecture, art, and merchandise.[^239] [^30] [^100] *Tetris* has been frequently referenced in pop culture, such as in *[The Simpsons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Simpsons "The Simpsons")*, *[The Big Bang Theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Big_Bang_Theory "The Big Bang Theory")*,[^100] *[Family Guy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Family_Guy "Family Guy")*, *[Futurama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Futurama "Futurama")*, *[Office Space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Office_Space "Office Space")*,[^240] *[Muppet Babies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muppet_Babies_\(1984_TV_series\) "Muppet Babies (1984 TV series)")*, and [Monty Python](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monty_Python "Monty Python").[^241] The game has also earned multiple Guinness records, such as the record for "largest architectural video game display", granted to a game of *Tetris* hosted on the side of the 29-story [Cira Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cira_Center "Cira Center") in April 2014.[^242] "Korobeiniki", a Russian folk song, has become widely associated with *Tetris* following its inclusion in the Game Boy version.[^162] A 1992 [Eurodance cover of "Korobeiniki"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tetris_\(Doctor_Spin_song\) "Tetris (Doctor Spin song)") by Doctor Spin peaked at number six on the UK singles charts.[^30] [^243] The " [*Tetris* effect](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tetris_effect "Tetris effect") " refers to the phenomena of perceiving certain patterns in dreams and mental images following engagement in a repetitive activity such as playing *Tetris.*[^244] [^245] The term was coined by Jeffrey Goldsmith in a 1994 article for *[Wired](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wired_\(magazine\) "Wired (magazine)")*,[^246] in which he compared *Tetris* to an "electronic drug".[^247]

The background of *Tetris*, including its creation and legal battles in the late 1980s, has been documented multiple times.[^248] [David Sheff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Sheff "David Sheff") provided a comprehensive overview in his influential Nintendo history book *[Game Over](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Game_Over_\(Sheff_book\) "Game Over (Sheff book)")* (1994). Subsequent books that covered this topic include the non-fiction books [Steven L. Kent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steven_L._Kent "Steven L. Kent") 's *[The Ultimate History of Video Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Ultimate_History_of_Video_Games "The Ultimate History of Video Games")* (2001), Tristan Donovan's *[Replay: The History of Video Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Replay:_The_History_of_Video_Games "Replay: The History of Video Games")* (2010), [Dan Ackerman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dan_Ackerman "Dan Ackerman") 's *The Tetris Effect* (2016), and the graphic novel [Box Brown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Box_Brown "Box Brown") 's *[Tetris: The Games People Play](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tetris:_The_Games_People_Play "Tetris: The Games People Play")* (2016).[^249] [^248] *Tetris* has also been the subject of documentaries including [BBC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BBC "BBC") 's *Tetris: From Russia with Love* (2004) and the independent *[Ecstasy of Order: The Tetris Masters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ecstasy_of_Order:_The_Tetris_Masters "Ecstasy of Order: The Tetris Masters")* (2010).[^249] [^248] *[Tetris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tetris_\(film\) "Tetris (film)")*, a film dramatization of the legal battles starring [Taron Egerton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taron_Egerton "Taron Egerton") as Rogers, premiered on [Apple TV+](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apple_TV%2B "Apple TV+") on March 31, 2023,[^245] to positive reviews, according to [Metacritic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metacritic "Metacritic"), and a viewership of 88,000 people, according to [Samba TV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samba_TV "Samba TV").[^250] [^251] Henk Rogers' memoir, *The Perfect Game—Tetris: From Russia With Love*, was published on April 1, 2025, to provide his perspective on *Tetris* 's history following the film's release.[^252] [^253]

*Tetris* is part of the competitive gaming scene, especially around the [NES version](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tetris_\(NES_video_game\) "Tetris (NES video game)").[^254] Competitor [Jonas Neubauer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jonas_Neubauer "Jonas Neubauer") and his victory in the inaugural [Classic Tetris World Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Classic_Tetris_World_Championship "Classic Tetris World Championship") (CTWC) in 2010 were the subjects of *Ecstasy of Order: The Tetris Masters*,[^255] which helped popularized competitive gameplay of *Tetris*.[^233] Competitors at the CTWC, typically adolescents, have used the CTWC to demonstrate advancements in the gameplay of the NES version. For example, gameplay techniques such as ["hypertapping" and "rolling"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tetris_\(NES_video_game\)#Speed_techniques "Tetris (NES video game)") have been used to help competitors to maximize their scores beyond level 29, which was previously deemed impossible to complete due to its speed.[^256] [^257] [Willis Gibson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Willis_Gibson "Willis Gibson") "beat" Tetris by playing NES *Tetris* [until it crashed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tetris_\(NES_video_game\)#Technical_problems_at_very_high_levels "Tetris (NES video game)") in a 40-minute livestream in January 2024, receiving significant media coverage for his achievement.[^258] [^259]

### Research

The *Tetris* game has frequently been featured in academic research, including in [psychology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Psychology "Psychology"), [computer science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_science "Computer science"), and [game studies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Game_studies "Game studies").[^12] By 2014, John K. Lindstedt and [Wayne D. Gray](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wayne_D._Gray "Wayne D. Gray"), cognitive scientists of the [Rensselaer Polytechnic Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rensselaer_Polytechnic_Institute "Rensselaer Polytechnic Institute"), had traced 133 scholarly papers across a variety of academic fields that utilized *Tetris* in their research.[^260] Soviet clinical psychologist [Vladimir Pokhilko](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vladimir_Pokhilko "Vladimir Pokhilko") was the first to use *Tetris* in clinical experiments, conducting them c. 1985 at the Moscow Medical Center.[^244] [^12] Prior to studying them, Pokhilko observed that distributed copies of *Tetris* to his colleagues impaired medical research due to their constant gameplay. Although he initially destroyed these copies, after new copies were reintroduced to his facility, Pokhilko used *Tetris* in psychological tests of his patients.[^261]

In psychology, starting with the research of American psychologist [Richard J. Haier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_J._Haier "Richard J. Haier") in 1992,[^262] [^263] [^264] *Tetris* has been frequently used in [neuroimaging](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neuroimaging "Neuroimaging") studies testing how gameplay affects the human brain.[^265] [^260] For example, the [near-transfer effects](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cognitive_training "Cognitive training") of *Tetris* on [mental rotation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mental_rotation "Mental rotation") is frequently researched, though research methods have varied widely and results have been contradictory.[^266] Furthermore, *Tetris* has been studied as a potential form of psychological intervention, particularly regarding [post-traumatic stress disorder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Post-traumatic_stress_disorder "Post-traumatic stress disorder") (PTSD).[^265] To date, playing *Tetris* as a valid form of imagery treatment for symptoms of PTSD is in an experimental stage and in need of further research. Nonetheless, several systematic reviews and meta-analyses of studies have demonstrated some evidence supporting its effectiveness in reducing intrusive memories, especially when combined with other interventions.[^267] [^268] [^269]

In computer science, the *Tetris* game has been the subject of research papers analyzing how the tetrominoes affect gameplay.[^270] Most analyze a traditional game of *Tetris* and do not account for features such as lookahead.[^271] In 1992, John Brzustowski argued in his [master's thesis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Master%27s_thesis "Master's thesis") for the [University of Waterloo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Waterloo "University of Waterloo") that it is impossible to play a traditional game of *Tetris* indefinitely,[^272] [^273] a conclusion affirmed by Heidi Burgiel of the [University of Minnesota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Minnesota "University of Minnesota") in 1997, who attributed the inevitable end to the game's Z-shaped tetrominoes and calculated a hard cap of seventy thousand tetrominoes.[^274] [^275] Partly accounting for the lookahead feature, in 2003 a trio of [MIT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MIT "MIT") students proved that the optimal strategy for playing a game of *Tetris* is [NP-complete](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NP-complete "NP-complete"), meaning it is difficult to be solved by an algorithm within a reasonable time due to the [game's complexity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computational_complexity_theory "Computational complexity theory"). This is true even if the player knew the complete sequence of incoming pieces.[^276] [^277] [^278]
