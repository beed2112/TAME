***Centipede*** is a 1981 [fixed shooter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fixed_shooter "Fixed shooter") [video game](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Video_game "Video game") developed and published by [Atari, Inc.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atari,_Inc. "Atari, Inc.") for [arcades](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arcade_video_game "Arcade video game").[^12] Designed by [Dona Bailey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dona_Bailey "Dona Bailey") and [Ed Logg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ed_Logg "Ed Logg"), it was one of the most commercially successful games from the [golden age of arcade video games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_age_of_arcade_video_games "Golden age of arcade video games"), and one of the first widely [played by women and girls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Women_and_video_games "Women and video games"). The primary objective is to shoot all the segments of a centipede that winds down the playing field.

*Centipede* was ported to Atari's own [2600](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atari_2600 "Atari 2600"), [5200](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atari_5200 "Atari 5200"), [7800](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atari_7800 "Atari 7800"), and [8-bit computers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atari_8-bit_computers "Atari 8-bit computers"). Under the [Atarisoft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atarisoft "Atarisoft") label, the game was sold for the [Apple II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apple_II "Apple II"), [Commodore 64](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commodore_64 "Commodore 64"), [ColecoVision](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ColecoVision "ColecoVision"), [VIC-20](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/VIC-20 "VIC-20"), [IBM PC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IBM_PC "IBM PC") (as a [self-booting disk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Self-booting_disk "Self-booting disk")), [Intellivision](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intellivision "Intellivision"), and [TI-99/4A](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TI-99/4A "TI-99/4A").[^13] [Superior Software](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superior_Software "Superior Software") published the port for the [BBC Micro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BBC_Micro "BBC Micro"). Versions for the [Game Boy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Game_Boy "Game Boy") and [Game Boy Color](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Game_Boy_Color "Game Boy Color") were also produced, as well as a version for the short-lived [Game.com](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Game.com "Game.com"). Other developers released more than 25 *Centipede* [clones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Video_game_clone "Video game clone") for home computers. It was followed by the arcade sequel *[Millipede](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Millipede_\(video_game\) "Millipede (video game)")* in 1982. In 1998, a [3D remake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Centipede_\(1998_video_game\) "Centipede (1998 video game)") for [Windows](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Windows "Windows"), [Mac](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mac_\(computer\) "Mac (computer)"), [PlayStation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PlayStation_\(console\) "PlayStation (console)"), and [Dreamcast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dreamcast "Dreamcast") was published by [Hasbro Interactive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hasbro_Interactive "Hasbro Interactive").

## Gameplay

![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/en/8/80/Centipede_arcade.png)

A new game, with the centipede at the top, the Bug Blaster at the bottom and a spider in the lower right

The player controls the small insect-like creature called the Bug Blaster. It is moved around the bottom area of the screen with a [trackball](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trackball "Trackball"), and fires small [darts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dart_\(missile\) "Dart (missile)") at a segmented [centipede](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Centipede "Centipede") advancing from the top of the screen through a field of [mushrooms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mushroom "Mushroom"). Each segment of the centipede becomes a mushroom when shot; shooting one of the middle segments splits the centipede into two pieces at that point. Each piece then continues independently on its way down the screen, with the rear piece sprouting its own head. If the centipede head is destroyed, the segment behind it becomes the next head. Shooting the head is worth 100 points while the other segments are 10. The centipede starts at the top of the screen, traveling either left or right. When it touches a mushroom or reaches the edge of the screen, it descends one level and reverses direction. The player can destroy mushrooms (a point each) by shooting them, but each takes four shots to destroy. At higher levels, the screen can become increasingly crowded with mushrooms due to player/enemy actions, causing the centipede to descend more rapidly.[^14]

![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/60/Centipede_Arcade_Automat.jpg/500px-Centipede_Arcade_Automat.jpg)

Upright arcade cabinet showing the white trackball controller

Once the centipede reaches the bottom of the screen, it stays within the player area and one-segment "head" centipedes will periodically appear from the side. This continues until the player has eliminated both the original centipede and all heads. When all the centipede's segments are destroyed, another one enters from the top of the screen. The initial centipede is 10 or 12 segments long, including the head; each successive centipede is one segment shorter and accompanied by one detached, faster-moving head. This pattern continues until all segments are separate heads, after which it repeats with a single full-length centipede.

The player also encounters other creatures besides the centipedes. [Fleas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flea "Flea") drop vertically and disappear upon touching the bottom of the screen, occasionally leaving a trail of mushrooms in their path when only a few mushrooms are in the player movement area; they are worth 200 points each and take two shots to destroy. [Spiders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spider "Spider") move across the player area in a zig-zag pattern and eat some of the mushrooms; they are worth 300, 600, or 900 points depending on the range they are shot from. [Scorpions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scorpion "Scorpion") move horizontally across the screen, turning every mushroom they touch into poison mushrooms. Scorpions are also worth the most points of all enemies with 1,000 points each. A centipede touching a poison mushroom will attack straight down toward the bottom, then return to normal behavior upon reaching it. This "poisoned" centipede can be both beneficial and detrimental to the player; the player can destroy them rapidly as it descends down, while at the same time, they can be very challenging to avoid, especially if already split into multiple segments.

The Bug Blaster is destroyed when hit by any enemy, after which any poisonous or partially damaged mushrooms revert to normal. 5 points are awarded for each regenerated mushroom. An extra life is awarded every 12,000 points.

## Development

[Dona Bailey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dona_Bailey "Dona Bailey") and [Ed Logg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ed_Logg "Ed Logg") developed *Centipede* for Atari.[^15] Logg, a supervisor, said that he did the design, while Bailey did about half of the programming.[^16] Bailey was one of the few [female game programmers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_women_in_the_video_game_industry "List of women in the video game industry") in the industry.[^17] Logg believed that its design was not biased by sex, unlike a fighting or sports game. Bailey said: "I really like pastels... I really wanted it to look different, to be visually arresting".[^18] [^16] Bailey had only recently discovered video games when she heard the song " [Space Invader](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pretenders_\(album\) "Pretenders (album)") " (1979) by [The Pretenders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Pretenders "The Pretenders") and then played *[Space Invaders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space_Invaders "Space Invaders")* (1978), but she was one of the few American women at the time with experience in [assembly language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assembly_language "Assembly language") programming.[^19]

## Reception

![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/5/57/Centipede_computer_game_for_Atari_8_bit_computers_1982.jpg)

Atari 8-bit computer cartridge (1982)

*Centipede* was one of the top four highest-grossing [arcade games of 1982](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1982_in_video_games "1982 in video games") in the United States, along with *[Ms. Pac-Man](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ms._Pac-Man "Ms. Pac-Man")*, *[Pac-Man](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pac-Man "Pac-Man")* and *[Donkey Kong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donkey_Kong_\(arcade_game\) "Donkey Kong (arcade game)")*.[^20] The Atari VCS port of *Centipede* sold 1,475,240 cartridges during 1982 to 1983, becoming the [11th-best-selling Atari game](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_best-selling_Atari_2600_video_games "List of best-selling Atari 2600 video games").[^21]

It was also one of the first coin-operated [arcade video games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arcade_video_game "Arcade video game") to have a significant female player base.[^22] [^23] *How to Win Video Games* (1982) estimated that half of its players and 60% of *[Pac-Man](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pac-Man "Pac-Man")* ' s were women, while 95% of *[Defender](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Defender_\(1981_video_game\) "Defender (1981 video game)")* players were men.[^24]

In 1983, *[Softline](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Softline_\(magazine\) "Softline (magazine)")* readers named *Centipede* ninth on the magazine's Top Thirty list of Atari 8-bit programs by popularity.[^25] The game received the award for "1984 Best Computer Action Game" at the 5th annual [Arkie Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arkie_Awards "Arkie Awards") where the judges described it as "pack\[ing\] a real roundhouse punch", and suggested that some "insist that \[the *Centipede*\] Atari cartridge is the best home-arcade edition you can buy".[^26]<sup><span title="Page / location: 28">: 28 </span></sup>  [David H. Ahl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_H._Ahl "David H. Ahl") of *[Creative Computing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Creative_Computing "Creative Computing") Video & Arcade Games* said that the Atari 5200 version was "delightful fun".[^27]

A 1982 [*Games*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Games_\(magazine\) "Games (magazine)") review of the 5200 version of the game stated that it is a faithful and satisfactory port of the arcade version, despite hardware limitations leading to a decrease in the amount of mushroom obstacles and a lack of trackball controls.[^28]

In a 1984 *[Video](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Video_\(magazine\) "Video (magazine)")* review of the Apple II version of the game, [Bill Kunkel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bill_Kunkel_\(journalist\) "Bill Kunkel (journalist)") and [Arnie Katz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arnie_Katz "Arnie Katz") commented that "the graphic limits of the Apple crimp the style" and expressed disappointment in the game's "sluggish" interfacing with trackball controllers.[^29]

In 1995, *[Flux](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flux_\(magazine\) "Flux (magazine)")* magazine ranked the arcade version 15th on their "Top 100 Video Games".[^30] In 1996, *[Next Generation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Next_Generation_\(magazine\) "Next Generation (magazine)")* listed the arcade version as number 84 on their "Top 100 Games of All Time", praising the cool concept, trackball control, and that it is accessible enough that "any human on the planet can play it well enough to enjoy it" yet "hard enough that even excellent gamers find it challenging".[^31]

In 2020, [The Strong National Museum of Play](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Strong_National_Museum_of_Play "The Strong National Museum of Play") inducted *Centipede* to its [World Video Game Hall of Fame](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Video_Game_Hall_of_Fame "World Video Game Hall of Fame").[^32]

## Legacy

### Re-releases

- The game is included in *[Arcade Classics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arcade_Classics "Arcade Classics")* for the [Sega Genesis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sega_Genesis "Sega Genesis") and [Game Gear](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Game_Gear "Game Gear") and a similar [Master System](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Master_System "Master System") compilation titled *[Arcade Smash Hits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arcade_Smash_Hits "Arcade Smash Hits")*.
- The game was released for [Microsoft Windows](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microsoft_Windows "Microsoft Windows") 3.x and [Mac OS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mac_OS "Mac OS") as part of the *[Microsoft Arcade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microsoft_Arcade "Microsoft Arcade")* package in 1993.
- [Accolade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Accolade,_Inc. "Accolade, Inc.") released a version for the [Game Boy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Game_Boy "Game Boy") in 1992. This port was re-released later by [Nintendo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nintendo "Nintendo") in 1995 with [Super Game Boy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Super_Game_Boy "Super Game Boy") support as part of their *[Arcade Classic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arcade_Classic "Arcade Classic")* series. This version is notable for having [background music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Background_music "Background music") in the title screen and different sound effects compared to the other ports.
- The game is included in the [Midway Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Midway_Games "Midway Games") -published *[Arcade's Greatest Hits: The Atari Collection 1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arcade%27s_Greatest_Hits:_The_Atari_Collection_1 "Arcade's Greatest Hits: The Atari Collection 1")* for the [Sega Saturn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sega_Saturn "Sega Saturn"), [Super Nintendo Entertainment System](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Super_Nintendo_Entertainment_System "Super Nintendo Entertainment System"), and the [PlayStation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PlayStation_\(console\) "PlayStation (console)").
- The arcade original was included in the PlayStation and [Dreamcast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dreamcast "Dreamcast") versions of the *[Centipede](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Centipede_\(1998_video_game\) "Centipede (1998 video game)")* 3D remake, replacing the arcade mode from the PC and Mac versions.
- It is also included in *Atari Arcade Hits 1* and *Atari Greatest Hits* for Windows, Atari Anniversary Advance for the [Game Boy Advance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Game_Boy_Advance "Game Boy Advance"), *[Atari Anniversary Edition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atari_Anniversary_Edition "Atari Anniversary Edition")* for the Dreamcast (*Redux* for the PlayStation) and *[Atari: 80 Classic Games in One!](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atari:_80_Classic_Games_in_One! "Atari: 80 Classic Games in One!")* for the PC.
- The game appears as a bonus unlockable minigame in the [PlayStation 2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PlayStation_2 "PlayStation 2") and [Xbox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xbox_\(console\) "Xbox (console)") versions of *[Terminator 3: Rise of the Machines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terminator_3:_Rise_of_the_Machines_\(video_game\) "Terminator 3: Rise of the Machines (video game)")*, that can be unlocked once the game is beaten.
- The game has also been made available for the Xbox and PlayStation 2 (with both arcade and Atari 2600 versions) as part of *[Atari Anthology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atari_Anthology "Atari Anthology")* in 2004.
- The [Xbox Live Arcade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xbox_Live_Arcade "Xbox Live Arcade") and [PlayStation Portable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PlayStation_Portable "PlayStation Portable") 's *Atari Arcade Classics* version was bundled with the sequel *Millipede*, which included an "evolution mode", featuring high-definition graphics and special effects like motion blur, trails, and particle-based explosions.[^33]
- The game was released via Xbox Live Arcade for the [Xbox 360](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xbox_360 "Xbox 360") on May 2, 2007.
- [Glu Mobile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glu_Mobile "Glu Mobile") released a licensed cellular phone version of *Centipede* that includes the original game as well as updated gameplay, skins, and modes.[^34]
- In 2008, Atari released the game for the [iPhone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IPhone "IPhone") and [iPod Touch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IPod_Touch "IPod Touch").
- The game is included in *[Retro Atari Classics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Retro_Atari_Classics "Retro Atari Classics")* and *[Atari Greatest Hits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atari_Greatest_Hits "Atari Greatest Hits") Volume 1* for the [Nintendo DS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nintendo_DS "Nintendo DS"). The former title also includes a remixed art version.
- Both the arcade and 2600 versions are part of *[Atari Vault](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atari_Vault "Atari Vault")* (2016) on [Steam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steam_\(service\) "Steam (service)").
- The Atari 2600 and Atari 7800 versions were released respectively for the [Evercade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evercade "Evercade") as part of Atari Collections 1 and 2 in 2020 as two out of ten launch cartridges for the system, while the arcade version was released as part of Atari Arcade 1 in 2021.
- The arcade, 2600 and 7800 versions were included in *[Atari 50](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atari_50 "Atari 50")* (2022) for the [Atari VCS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atari_VCS_\(2021_console\) "Atari VCS (2021 console)"), [Nintendo Switch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nintendo_Switch "Nintendo Switch"), [PlayStation 4](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PlayStation_4 "PlayStation 4"), [PlayStation 5](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PlayStation_5 "PlayStation 5"), [Windows](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Windows "Windows"), [Xbox One](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xbox_One "Xbox One"), and [Xbox Series X/S](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xbox_Series_X/S "Xbox Series X/S").

*Centipede* was followed by *[Millipede](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Millipede_\(video_game\) "Millipede (video game)")* in 1982.

In 1992, [Atari Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atari_Games "Atari Games") developed a [prototype](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prototype "Prototype") of an arcade game called *[Arcade Classics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arcade_Classics "Arcade Classics")* for their 20th anniversary, which includes *Missile Command 2* and *Super Centipede* with co-op 2-player mode.[^35]

A [3D](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/3D_computer_graphics "3D computer graphics") [fantasy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fantasy "Fantasy") [role-playing game](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Role-playing_game "Role-playing game") based on the original game was being developed by Dark Science for the [Jaguar CD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atari_Jaguar_CD "Atari Jaguar CD") under the working title *Centipede 2000*.[^36] The [source code](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Source_code "Source code") of the project no longer exists and the only remaining proof of its existence is a short [video clip](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Digital_video "Digital video") from the developer.[^37] [^38] [^39]

In 1997, *Centipede X* was in development for the [Nintendo 64](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nintendo_64 "Nintendo 64"), alongside *[Joust X](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joust_X "Joust X")* and *[Robotron X](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robotron_X "Robotron X")* by [Midway Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Midway_Games "Midway Games"), but it was not released.[^40] [^41] [^42]

In 1998, after acquiring the intellectual property of [Atari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atari "Atari") from then-owner [JT Storage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/JT_Storage "JT Storage"), [Hasbro Interactive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hasbro_Interactive "Hasbro Interactive") released [a new version of the game](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Centipede_\(1998_video_game\) "Centipede (1998 video game)") for Windows, [PlayStation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PlayStation_\(console\) "PlayStation (console)"), [Dreamcast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dreamcast "Dreamcast"), and [Mac](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mac_\(computer\) "Mac (computer)"). It is very different from the original: 3D graphics, free movement around the map, and a campaign which can be played in single-player or multiplayer mode.

In 2011, [Atari, SA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atari,_SA "Atari, SA") released *[Centipede: Infestation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Centipede:_Infestation "Centipede: Infestation")* for [Nintendo 3DS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nintendo_3DS "Nintendo 3DS") and [Wii](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wii "Wii").

In 2019, [ICE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Innovative_Concepts_in_Entertainment "Innovative Concepts in Entertainment") created a remaster of *Centipede* as *Centipede Chaos* for arcades.

In 2020, GameTaco released *Centipede: Cash Blast* for iOS.[^43]

A revamped version of the game, *Centipede: Recharged*, was released for Windows, [Nintendo Switch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nintendo_Switch "Nintendo Switch"), [PlayStation 4](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PlayStation_4 "PlayStation 4"), [PlayStation 5](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PlayStation_5 "PlayStation 5"), [Xbox One](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xbox_One "Xbox One"), [Xbox Series X/S](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xbox_Series_X_and_Series_S "Xbox Series X and Series S"), [Google Stadia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Google_Stadia "Google Stadia"), and [Atari VCS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atari_VCS_\(2021_console\) "Atari VCS (2021 console)") in September 2021 as part of the *[Atari Recharged](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atari_Recharged "Atari Recharged")* series.

*Centipede* appears in the film *[Pixels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pixels_\(2015_film\) "Pixels (2015 film)")*.[^62]

In May 2016, Emmett/Furla/Oasis Films closed with Atari to produce and finance both *Centipede* and *Missile Command* film adaptations.[^63]

[Dynamite Entertainment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dynamite_Entertainment "Dynamite Entertainment") started a limited run [comic book](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Comic_book "Comic book") series based on *Centipede* in July 2017. The series was written by [Max Bemis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Max_Bemis "Max Bemis").[^64]

[Lego](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lego "Lego") released a set based on the Atari 2600. Included is a cartridge for Centipede as well as a diorama showing the titular character.[^65]

## Competitive arena

The game was chosen for the final round of the 1981 Atari World Championships run by Tournament Games International. The men's champion was Eric Ginner and the women's champion was Ok-Soo Han.[^66]

The world record score on the arcade version of *Centipede* was 16,389,547 points by Jim Schneider of the USA on August 1, 1984.[^67]

Donald Hayes of [Windham, New Hampshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Windham,_New_Hampshire "Windham, New Hampshire"), scored a [world record](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_record "World record") 7,111,111 points under tournament rules on the arcade version of *Centipede* on November 5, 2000.[^68] [^69]
