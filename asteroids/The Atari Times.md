George</font> <b>September 7, 2001</b><p><b><font>F</font></b> ew games in history are as recognized as the Atari classic, <i>Asteroids</i>. The pounding sound effects, simple graphics, and strangely addictive gameplay has permeated throughout all of American society. One only has to watch TV shows like NBC's <i>Freaks and Geeks</i>, or the classic film <i>National Lampoon's Vacation</i> to realize just how entrenched this game has become in our popular culture.</p><table width="50" align="right"><tbody><tr><td width="100%"><a href="https://web.archive.org/web/20070927230614/http://www.ataritimes.com/images/arcade/asteroids.gif"><img src="https://web.archive.org/web/20070927230614im_/http://www.ataritimes.com/images/arcade/asteroids.gif" align="middle" width="320" height="240"></a></td></tr><tr><td><p align="center"><b><font>The one, the only, the original and the best, Asteroids by Atari.</font></b></p></td></tr></tbody></table><p>Not many games have been copied or ported as often as <i>Asteroids</i>.<i>Pac-Man</i> perhaps, or <i>Space Invaders</i>, would definitely be nearest competitors for the clone crown. Even today, <i>Asteroids</i> clones and ports are popping up as Java applets, or in current releases for modern game systems such as the Sega Dreamcast.</p><h4>History</h4><p>The idea of <i>Asteroids</i> was originally conceived by Lyle Raines who was Atari's vice president of engineering in 1979. Ed Logg, eventually took the lead on the project and is credited with it's programming.</p><p>The game's concept is simple: Shoot the slow moving large rocks into medium sized rocks. Shoot the medium sized rocks into even smaller rocks. The smaller the size of the rocks, the faster they move. This asteroid field puts the player in an ever-increasing danger zone of flying debris. The UFO's were added to spice things up and keep the player from just sitting there avoiding the asteroids.</p><p><i>Asteroids</i> was designed on the same hardware as another of Atari's classics: <i>Lunar Lander</i>. Both games used the high resolution, black and white vector-scan screens. Yet <i>Lunar Lander</i> never became the world-wide phenomena that <i>Asteroids</i> did. <i>Asteroids</i> was so popular when it was released, it replaced <i>Space Invaders</i> as the king of the arcades and gave Atari enough cash to be financially independent from Warner.</p><p>"Senior engineer Steve Calfee reflected that <i>Asteroids</i> appeals to some low, primitive drive in the human mind to clean and take control of the environment. Blasting asteroids into rubble until a once-crowded screen turned into a neat black field appealed to people whose lives were nothing but a field of chaos. For them, <i>Asteroids</i> became a metaphor for life." - <i>Paul Schuytema, Microsoft Arcade, The Official Strategy Guide.</i></p><table width="180" align="right"><tbody><tr><td width="100%"><a href="https://web.archive.org/web/20070927230614/http://www.ataritimes.com/images/arcade/asteroids_deluxe.gif"><img src="https://web.archive.org/web/20070927230614im_/http://www.ataritimes.com/images/arcade/asteroids_deluxe.gif" width="180" height="135"></a></td></tr><tr><td width="100%"><p align="center"><b><i>Asteroids Deluxe</i> removed the randomized hyperspace and included a shield instead.</b></p></td></tr><tr><td width="100%"></td></tr><tr><td width="100%"><a href="https://web.archive.org/web/20070927230614/http://www.ataritimes.com/images/arcade/spaceduel3.gif"><img src="https://web.archive.org/web/20070927230614im_/http://www.ataritimes.com/images/arcade/spaceduel3.gif" align="middle" width="180" height="135"></a></td></tr><tr><td width="100%"><p align="center"><b><i>Space Duel</i> added some strange new features.</b></p></td></tr><tr><td width="100%"></td></tr><tr><td width="100%"><a href="https://web.archive.org/web/20070927230614/http://www.ataritimes.com/images/arcade/blasteroids.gif"><img src="https://web.archive.org/web/20070927230614im_/http://www.ataritimes.com/images/arcade/blasteroids.gif" align="middle" width="180" height="135"></a></td></tr><tr><td width="100%"><p align="center"><b><i>Blasteroids</i> moved the series from vector to raster.</b></p></td></tr><tr><td width="100%"></td></tr><tr><td width="100%"><a href="https://web.archive.org/web/20070927230614/http://www.ataritimes.com/images/arcade/spacefury.gif"><img src="https://web.archive.org/web/20070927230614im_/http://www.ataritimes.com/images/arcade/spacefury.gif" align="middle" width="180" height="135"></a></td></tr><tr><td width="100%"><p align="center"><b><i>Space Fury</i> by Sega. Down and dirty blasting action.</b></p></td></tr></tbody></table><h4>Sequels, Variants, and Clones</h4><p>Atari tried to improve on the original <i>Asteroids</i> game introducing <i>Asteroids Deluxe</i> in 1980. This version of the game offered animated graphics, better sound, and more in-depth gameplay. It also included a color backdrop to make the game more interesting to play.</p><p>Also in 1980, Atari released <i>Space Duel</i> which included multi-player capability, complex physics, improved sound effects, and, most importantly, color vector graphics. Unfortunately, <i>Space Duel</i> was never as popular or as well known as the original primarily due to the strangeness of the objects being blasted. No longer was the player fighting an endless armada of asteroids and UFOs, but a strange assortment of geometric shapes. People used to rock-blasting didn't know what to make of the new game.</p><p>Atari's most recent <i>Asteroids</i> revision came in 1987 from Atari Games. <i>Blasteroids</i> did away with the vector screens in favor of more modern raster images. <i>Blasteroids</i> added morphing ships, power-ups, and highly detailed images.</p><p>Then there is <i>Space Fury</i>, Sega's answer to <i>Asteroids</i>. This game took all of the familiar concepts and melted them together into a very unique action game. Unlike the slow, methodical pace of <i>Asteroids</i>, <i>Space Fury</i> is a pure action game. Your adversary is a one-eyed alien taunting you at every step. Who could forget his condescending statement he makes when you plunk your quarters into the machine: "So, a creature for MY amusement!"</p><h4>Home Versions</h4><p>There are too many home versions of <i>Asteroids</i>, to be able to catalog them all! From official versions to shareware clones, the game has managed to appear on practically every video game console and computer in one form or another!</p><p><i>Asteroids</i> for the Atari 2600 was quite an accomplishment as it was the first 2600 game to reach a whopping 8K in size! This is quite a feat considering that the 2600 was designed to only address 4K of memory. While the 2600 version lacked the originals vector graphics, it did include the core gameplay and enough options (hyperspace, shields, flip, UFOs, easy or hard asteroids) to keep gamers happy at home.</p><p>Other versions of <i>Asteroids</i> include those for Atari systems such as the 7800, Lynx, 8-bit computer line, and the ST line. One important exception, is the Atari 5200. This version, identical to the 8-bit home computer versions, was never released and exists only as a prototype. Atari also released home versions of <i>Asteroids</i> through it's AtariSoft label for competing game and computer systems.</p><p>The Atari Jaguar, while never receiving an official <i>Asteroids</i> game did get some hobby versions of the game written. One of which can be found at</p><p><a href="https://web.archive.org/web/20070927230614/http://justclaws.atari.org/devcats/protos/ithink/">http://justclaws.atari.org/devcats/protos/ithink/</a></p><p>Some home clones of <i>Asteroids</i> include <i>Mine Storm</i> for the Vectrex, <i>Astro Smash</i> for the Intellivision (although some would call this one a stretch), and <i>Space Fury</i> for the ColecoVision.</p><div
